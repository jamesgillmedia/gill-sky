# u4-opus: what I understand before building

Written before the build, for Gill to read. u4-opus is a parallel version to compare against u3. It is not a replacement.

## 1. Who Gill is as an artist

Gill James has one practice, not a camera job with hobbies on the side. When he films a music video, throws a joio cup, draws Spirit Eye with Joshua Ngenda, photographs a cup in a hand, or plays cello as Lost Coffee, it is the same eye and the same hands. That eye is artful and the hands are practical, so the work is always both a picture and an object that does something. More and more, the work leaves the hand and enters the land: places to stand inside, listen and look. He is Crəə-Métis, based in Vancouver, and the poem says it best: all his relations, in all his creations.

For the site, that means the five chapters sit side by side in one sky as equals. Sound and objects are not side pages. The sky is only the path between works. Once you leave the sky, the work owns the phone. If the stars are louder than the picture, I've lost the plot.

## 2. What the asymmetry means, and how I'll show it

There are two kinds of asymmetry, and both are on purpose.

**Unequal depth.** Film has the most footage, so it goes deepest. Every other medium gets at least four levels and a real bottom. I won't pad a chapter to match Film, and I won't cut Film to match the others. Where a level has no media, it says so quietly instead of inventing something.

**Off-centre layouts.** Nothing is a centred grid of equal circles; v1 failed because everything looked like the same circles. The grammar is the same everywhere: pinch in to go deeper, pinch out to go back exactly one level, and every zoom grows from the spot you touched. A tap also goes one level deeper on the thing you touched, because tap-only navigation was rejected, not tapping. But each medium develops in its own way, like a print coming up in the tray:

- Film moves in 2.39 strips that are offset and float at different depths.
- Photo uses prints of mixed ratios, slightly rotated, in loose stacks.
- Sound uses horizontal waveform bands, and stars only frame the music.
- Objects are cut-out photos with soft shadows on an off-kilter grid.
- About is led by text.

In practice: board cards get unequal sizes and offsets from a fixed pattern per medium, never random. Each deep level has one clear focal frame, and the rest falls away in size, blur and dimness.

**The board level comes back.** The board level is what u3 skipped. In every chapter the order is: the sky, then the chapter's constellation (names turning into thumbnails where they sit), then the chapter's board, then a piece, then deeper. The breadcrumb names every level. There is one back button, and it goes up exactly one level. A tap on a chapter never jumps straight to a full-bleed piece.

## 3. Each medium in u4-opus

I count levels from the sky. Sky = 0, the chapter's constellation = 1, the board = 2. The number in each heading is the deepest level.

**Film: 7 levels for most films, 8 for Weird Kid.**
1. The film constellation: labelled stars that become small 2.39 thumbnails where they sit.
2. The board: fewer, bigger 2.39 cards, offset left and right at different depths so it breathes. Each card shows a title, the role and type line, and the length when the file gives one. Cards on screen play their short muted preview loops. A "list" flip gives pro2's dense text list.
3. The piece: an off-centre 2.39 player with the published credits as a list. This is the native player from pro2/Scope, with the real file and no YouTube.
4. Filmstrip bands: sprocketed bands with a scrub rail, live frame thumbnails and a shot strip.
5. The scatter: an asymmetric scatter of frames at mixed sizes, with clips playing in place.
6. The push: into the frame you touched, until it is full-bleed.
7. Credits as a list on the still.
8. Weird Kid only: Kiesza and the neighbouring frames before the credits.

**Photo: 6 levels.**
1. The photo constellation.
2. The board: loose stacks of rotated, mixed-ratio prints.
3. One print, off-centre, with its stack falling away behind it.
4. The contact sheet the print sits in.
5. That print full-bleed, with the slow push.
6. Grain crops of that same print, the darkroom bottom.

No titles are invented. Untitled prints stay unlabelled.

**Sound: 6 levels.**
1. The sound constellation: the dotted waveform star chart, the only place stars frame work. A small player rail sits on it, so a song is one tap from the Sound constellation.
2. The board: offset horizontal waveform bands, each with its cover, its song name, the length from the file, and a play button that plays in place.
3. The song: the sound-sketch player you liked, with the dark rail, the mint line and the measured waveform.
4. Inside the waveform, with the near-lens bokeh closeness of the water plunge.
5. Among the stars as they move with the audio.
6. The bottom: the wind-chime sketch. One finger gives a gentle tilt or drift, never an orbit you can get lost in.

**Objects: 5 levels.**
1. The objects constellation: small cut-outs of the real stills.
2. The board: cut-out object photos with soft shadows on an off-kilter grid. The joio cup opens it.
3. The object close-up (a cup or a paddle).
4. Process, glaze and detail. Where a piece has no process pictures, the level says "no process pictures for this piece".
5. A full-bleed joio clip in motion, with Spirit Eye beside it. Spirit Eye uses its exact wording: "recognized with Best Public Art Concept and Council's Choice within the BIDC Public Art Intensive", co-created with Joshua Ngenda.

**About: 10 levels.**
1. The about constellation.
2. The board, text-led and set off-centre: GILL JAMES, Cinematographer + Artist + Spatial Designer, Vancouver. Below that are the poem, the bio, contact, joio.co and Hire, plus the client line as it stands today (BMW · Coca-Cola · AXE · Band-Aid).
3. to 9. The calculator with its seven layers, the glitch building with each pinch. Layer 7 is the final bio, word for word, as selectable text.
10. The moon grid.

Pinching out from the first calculator layer goes back to the board, one level, rather than straight to the sky.

**Rules across every medium:**
- Motion is identical with Reduce Motion on.
- Real media only, with no YouTube.
- Only the approved family photos are used.
- It keeps working with JavaScript off.
- It is an installable app with its own manifest, service-worker scope and cache, and it works offline once visited.

## 4. Where I disagree, or would push further

- **Density versus breathing.** "pro2 density" and "the Film board breathes with fewer, bigger cards" pull against each other. I'll keep the board itself asymmetric and calm, and put pro2's density in two places: the always-visible caption lines (title, role, length, a count line at the top), and a list flip on every board, which is the "zoom sky as home, list as an optional flip" you liked in the Studio board. If you want the board itself denser, it is one number per medium to change.
- **One more stop.** Putting the constellation level and the board level back to back adds a stop before the work. I'll make the constellation stop worth it (names become thumbnails in place, plus the sound rail), and a second tap or pinch moves straight on. If it still feels slow on the phone, I'd merge them and let the board bloom in place.
- **The top bar.** Keeping the shortcut glyphs, Reel, Hire, List and Contact all in one bar is part of why it covered full-bleed pictures in u3. Below the sky, I'll keep only the back button and the breadcrumb, and push the work down beneath a solid strip rather than under a gradient.
- **The dark band on About.** The dark band was added in u3 to make the text readable, and you've called it a bug. I'll use text shadow and a lighter wash on the calculator instead. If the green display eats a word, I'll move the text rather than bring the band back.
- **Layer 4 of About.** Layer 4 is your own artist bio from gilljames.com ("∞ ᑕᐣᓯ James Gill is a Canadian indigenous…"). I'm keeping it exactly as written. It differs from the final public bio in name order and wording, so it's worth a look.
- **Pushing further.** I'd like each board to remember where you were when you come back up, so back lands on the card you left from rather than at the top. I'd also like a card to light the matching star in the minimap, so the sky stays the path between works even inside a board.
